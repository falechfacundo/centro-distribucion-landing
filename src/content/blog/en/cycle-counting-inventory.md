Cycle counting verifies part of the inventory on a recurring schedule instead of waiting for a full stocktake. To produce reliable information, define what to count, how to record discrepancies and who can approve adjustments.

## Segment inventory

You can organize counts by location, category, turnover, value or criticality. ABC analysis is one option: higher-impact items are counted more often. The rule should fit your operational risk and pace.

## Prepare the count

Before starting, agree on:

- Which locations and products are included.
- Whether movements in that area will pause during the task.
- Which unit of measure should be counted.
- How lots, serials, damage and expiry dates will be handled.

Clear instructions prevent comparing quantities that use different units.

## Record results without erasing history

Record the physical quantity, expected quantity and discrepancy. Keep who counted, when, where and why an adjustment is proposed. A traceable record makes it possible to review causes and compare future counts.

## Investigate before adjusting

A discrepancy may come from an unclosed receipt, a movement linked to another location, an incorrect unit conversion or a counting error. Review related movements before confirming an adjustment.

## Measure causes, not just accuracy

Alongside the total discrepancy, look for repeated patterns across products, shifts, zones or movement types. This information can help improve receiving, putaway, labeling and training.
