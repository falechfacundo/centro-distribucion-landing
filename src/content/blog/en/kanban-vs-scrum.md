Kanban and Scrum are the two most widely used agile methods for organizing a team’s work. They are often presented as rivals, but they actually solve different problems. Choosing well depends less on trends and more on how work reaches your team.

## What is Kanban

Kanban is a visual, continuous method. Work is represented on a board with columns (for example **To do**, **In progress** and **Done**) and each task is a card that moves from left to right.

Its core ideas are simple:

- **Visualize** all the work in one place.
- **Limit work in progress**, so you don’t start more than you can finish.
- **Improve flow** continuously by looking at where tasks get stuck.

There are no fixed cycles or mandatory roles: tasks come in and go out when appropriate.

## What is Scrum

Scrum organizes work into **sprints**, fixed-length cycles (usually one to four weeks). At the start of each sprint the team picks which backlog tasks it commits to completing, and at the end it reviews what it achieved and how to improve.

Scrum brings structure: planning, review and retrospective meetings, plus roles such as Product Owner and Scrum Master.

## Key differences

| | Kanban | Scrum |
|---|---|---|
| Cadence | Continuous flow | Fixed-length sprints |
| Priority changes | Any time | Ideally between sprints |
| Main metric | Cycle time and flow | Velocity and sprint completion |
| Roles | Not prescribed | Product Owner, Scrum Master, team |

## When to choose Kanban

Kanban works best when work arrives **unpredictably or continuously**: support, maintenance, operations, marketing or any team that receives requests all the time. It is also an excellent entry point if your team is just starting to get organized.

## When to choose Scrum

Scrum shines when you build a **product with stage-based goals** and need commitment, rhythm and predictability. Short cycles force you to decide what matters most and deliver value regularly.

## You don’t have to pick just one

Many teams combine both: they use sprints to plan and a Kanban board to see daily progress, with work-in-progress limits. What matters is that the method serves the team, not the other way around.

## Putting it into practice

Start with the minimum: a board with three columns and your current tasks. If your team needs rhythm, add sprints; if it needs more context, add epics and goals.
