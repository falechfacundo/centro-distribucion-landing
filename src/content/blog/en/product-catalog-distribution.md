A reliable product catalog reduces confusion across purchasing, receiving, warehousing and inventory. Before automating processes, agree on what identifies each item and who maintains its data.

## Define a stable identifier

Each product needs a unique internal code that does not depend on its commercial description. The code should remain stable when names, suppliers or packaging change. If you also use barcodes or manufacturer references, store them as related identifiers rather than replacements for the internal code.

## Separate product, packaging and unit

A case of twelve and a single sales unit may refer to the same product but represent different quantities. Define clearly:

- The base inventory unit.
- Packaging types and their equivalents.
- The unit used to purchase, receive and dispatch goods.
- Conversion and rounding rules.

Agreeing on these rules prevents discrepancies when a supplier invoices by case and the warehouse counts individual units.

## Establish data ownership

Assign a source of truth for description, category, barcode, unit, dimensions and tax data. Avoid letting each team create its own variant. A simple review step can catch duplicates before they affect orders or movements.

## Prepare product data for warehouse work

Alongside the commercial description, the catalog can include information useful for receiving and storage: weight, dimensions, storage conditions, lot or serial details where applicable, and dates that need tracking.

Not every product needs the same attributes. Define which data is required by category and which is optional.

## Start with a representative sample

Before migrating the full catalog, test products across categories, units and packaging. Check for duplicate codes, valid conversions and whether warehouse and purchasing teams can find items using their usual terms.
